    Getting started

    1. Type anyone's name and press Block. FaceBlock finds public photos of them on its own — you don't need to upload anything.
    2. Check the faces it found and press Confirm. If one is the wrong person, untick it first. Nothing is saved until you confirm — even when the photos come from FaceBlock's own reference set.
    3. Keep browsing. Their face gets covered wherever it appears on any website — in photos and in videos. Come back here to unblock someone, refresh their photos if it starts missing them, or flip the switch above to pause everything.

    Good to know

    • Nothing is uploaded. Only the name you type is sent out, to look for public photos. Your browsing, the pages you visit, and the faces it matches all stay on this device.
    • No photos are kept. FaceBlock remembers the person, not the pictures — just the numbers it needs to recognise them.
    • It works best for public figures — people with photos on Wikipedia or Wikimedia. If it can't find someone, it says so rather than guessing. Blocking a private individual may not work.
    • It isn't perfect yet. It can miss faces that are very small, blurry, or turned almost fully sideways, and it can occasionally cover the wrong person. This is an early release.
    • Photos can go stale. If someone isn't covered at all — no mask appears, even though they're on screen — press Refresh reference photos on their entry below (or block them again) to gather a fresh set. Include shots that match their current hair and facial hair if those have changed. You'll review the new photos before anything changes.
    • Hair or beard outside the box? If you already see a mask on their face but hair or facial hair still shows at the edges, the match worked — the box is just too tight. Refreshing reference photos won't fix that; coverage is adjusted separately from recognition.
